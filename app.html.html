    What it does, every cycle

    1. Listen for 1.5 s. The speaker stays silent so the device doesn't hear itself.
    2. Predict the female tone from air temperature: about 400 Hz at 26 °C, plus 10 Hz per °C.
    3. Find peaks in the female band and the male band (about 1.5 × female) with an FFT.
    4. Check for the duet: 3 × female ≈ 2 × male, within the tolerance. That shared harmonic is the pairing signal.
    5. Jam for 3 s with a rapidly wobbling tone and two side tones around that harmonic, which creates beats that bury it. Then listen again.

    Simulated wingbeats are synthetic tones built from published values (female about 400 Hz, male about 600 Hz, duet near 1200 Hz; Cator et al., 2009) and a 10 Hz/°C shift (published range 8–13). The simulated mosquitoes don't react to the jamming, so this demo shows detection and masking, not proof that mating drops.
